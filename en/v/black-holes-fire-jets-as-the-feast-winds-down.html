Black Holes Fire Jets as the Feast Winds Down

Some black holes throw part of their meal back out, as a narrow beam shooting from their poles at nearly the speed of light. That's a jet. And surprisingly, it may appear not while the black hole is feasting, but as the feast winds down.

Researchers followed stars that wandered too close to giant black holes and were torn apart. A feast that lasts a few years.

The jets switched on late, once the feeding rate had dropped to about two or three percent of the most a black hole can take in.

Small black holes, born from single stars, seem to switch on their jets at about the same point.

The sample is small, and the feeding rate wasn't measured directly. But if it holds, black holes millions of times apart in mass may follow the same rule.
