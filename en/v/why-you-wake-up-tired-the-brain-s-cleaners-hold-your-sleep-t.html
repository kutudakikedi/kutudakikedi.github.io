Why You Wake Up Tired: The Brain's 'Cleaners' Hold Your Sleep Together

Some nights you sleep enough hours, and still wake up exhausted. The problem isn't how long you slept. It's that your sleep was broken into pieces.

Something keeps sleep in one piece, and for years we assumed it was neurons. It isn't. The brain has maintenance cells called microglia. We thought they were just the cleaning crew.

A team built a microscope small enough to sit on a mouse's head, and watched these cells live while the animals roamed freely. Every time the brain slipped from waking into sleep, the microglia responded instantly.

The signal comes through a familiar door. Adenosine, the same molecule coffee blocks to keep you awake. The researchers shut that door only in the microglia. The mice still slept, but their sleep fell apart, flicking between waking and deep sleep all night.

So waking up tired isn't always about sleeping too little. Microglia don't start your sleep. They hold it together once it begins.
